Fox Valley Forge SC · Identity directions · Draft for review

The Forge Cup

Five directions for the club's new spring tournament, each explored by two image models, Google Gemini and OpenAI, with the reasoning behind every one and a clear recommendation.

The brief

The Forge Cup is a spring tournament for boys and girls U8–U19, open to every club. The mark has to say Fox Valley Forge to the families who already know the club, and say this is a serious event worth traveling to to the ones who don't.

The raw material is already in the club's name and crest: the forge (fire, anvil, hammer, spark), the fox from the club crest, and the cup itself. The palette stays in the family: navy, crimson and gold.

What the mark has to do

  • 1Belong to the Forge family at a glance, without copying the club crest.
  • 2Stand on its own as an event: on a registration site, a banner, a medal.
  • 3Survive at 32 pixels (app icon, social avatar) and on embroidery.
  • 4Own one idea. A mark that says three things says nothing.

Recommendation

Fox Fire as the primary mark, Forged Trophy for medals and print

Fox Fire is the only direction that passes all four tests and could not belong to any other club. Forged Trophy is its formal partner wherever restraint matters more than impact. Directions 04 and 05 didn't hold up in either model.

01

Fox Fire

Recommended · primary mark

The idea

A single flame, split crimson and navy, with a fox in the negative space looking up at a gold ball rising like a spark.

Why it fits the Forge

The forge is fire. The club crest is a fox. The tournament is the ball. One shape carries all three, and the hidden fox rewards a second look, which is what makes a mark ownable rather than generic. It is the only direction here that could not belong to any other club.

Verdict

OpenAI's version is the one to take forward. The two-tone split reads instantly as the club's colors, the fox is clean white negative space, and it holds as one silhouette at app-icon size. Gemini's adds extra flame tongues and colors the fox in, so it gets busy small. Refinement: tighten the fox's chest, drop the small sparkle, and set the full name in the club's typeface.

02

Forged Trophy

Recommended · medals, trophies, print

The idea

A single gold line draws the trophy. Its handles rise into fox ears, and a navy ball sits in the bowl. The name is set in widely spaced capitals beneath.

Why it fits the Forge

Major cup competitions lead with the trophy and with restraint. This is the version that looks right engraved on a winner's medal, stitched on a scarf, or printed small on a program cover. It pairs with Fox Fire as the formal lockup.

Verdict

OpenAI's version: elegant and premium; the ear-handles are a quiet detail. A thin line weakens below about an inch, which is why it supports Fox Fire rather than replacing it. Gemini's rim reads as a cat's head in a basket. Set it aside.

03

Anvil Shield

Alternate · patches & jerseys

The idea

A heraldic shield whose top edge is the silhouette of an anvil. A trophy and a gold ball sit inside, with the name on a crimson band.

Why it fits the Forge

It has the gravity of a European club crest, and the anvil top gives it a silhouette no other shield has. Best as an embroidered patch or a sleeve badge.

Verdict

OpenAI's version has the right silhouette, but the trophy and banner inside are generic. If chosen, the trophy should be built into the anvil rather than placed in front of it. Gemini sets the anvil on top as a separate object, which breaks the idea.

04

The Strike

Not recommended as drawn

The idea

A classic cup roundel. The ball is faceted like hammered metal, with a crimson spark where the hammer lands. The name runs around the ring.

Why it fits the Forge

The roundel is the most traditional cup format there is, and "the moment of impact" ties the forge to the goal. It is a solid, safe direction.

Verdict

In both versions the hammer is clumsy and fights the ring. OpenAI's also introduces gray, which is off-palette. Worth revisiting with no hammer, only the faceted ball and spark.

05

Hammer Monogram

Not recommended as drawn

The idea

An F built from a blacksmith's hammer, with a spark for the middle bar, set in a circle beside a stacked wordmark.

Why it fits the Forge

A lettermark gives the event a compact symbol for social avatars and merchandise.

Verdict

Neither model built the F. Both drew a literal hammer, which reads as hardware-store clip art. The idea only works hand-drawn; skip it in this round.

What happens after a pick

  1. The chosen direction is redrawn by hand as clean vector artwork. These images are concepts, not final files.
  2. The name is set in Oswald, the club's headline typeface, so the event matches every other Forge piece.
  3. The mark is tested at icon size, on embroidery, on navy and on white, and in one color.
  4. A delivery kit follows: square for GotSoccer, horizontal and stacked lockups, light and dark versions, PNG and SVG.
