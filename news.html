Club News  ·  From the President

Challenge Accepted

On the first day of our inaugural season, Mike Weyant sat down to record a message for every Forge family — on the five things he wants this club to stand for, everything being added this year, and why our season opens with a walk instead of a game.

Monday, August 24, 2026  ·  Mike Weyant, Club President

5 min 32 secPrefer to read? The full message is written out below.

Today we begin the inaugural season as Forge. A year ago this club did not exist — there were two clubs, two histories, two cultures, two sets of staff and two sets of facilities, and plenty of people who were not sure it could be done.

“It was mission impossible. Many people told me not to attempt this. Together we said challenge accepted.”

Forge is more than a soccer club. The aim is an environment where our community's families develop the character traits that make these kids successful on the field and in life. Mike built his message around five of them. They are not words on paper — they are the beginning of the story of how this club intends to behave.

One
Perseverance

Look at how far we've come. What starts today didn't exist a year ago, and there were moments in the process when even Mike wasn't sure it could be done.

“Perseverance means you don't quit something because it's difficult. You keep going because it's worth doing.”

One valley, one club. And it's just the start.

Two
Humility

Are we the best version of ourselves right now? Could we do better? Absolutely. Did we get everything right? Absolutely not — mistakes have been made and more are coming. Humility means being willing to learn, willing to listen, and willing to admit when you can do better. Be humble enough to learn and you earn the courage to attempt big things.

Here is what that courage is buying this year:

  • A significant amount of new staff
  • Futsal programming
  • 6v6 leagues
  • Strength, agility and quickness training
  • Nutritional programming for kids and adults
  • Adult fitness
  • Tutoring and homework help to support academics
  • The Forge Classic

“What will this club become if we are willing to build it?”

Three
Gratitude

Mike's personal favorite, and the one he asks families to take home. We would all be happier for a few minutes a day spent being thankful — and this is worth teaching your kids, because somebody is making sacrifices they don't yet understand.

Somebody is driving them. Somebody bought the cleats. Somebody took the 7am game assignment. Somebody arranged the team party.

“The kids owe you a huge hug. Go ask for it. Seriously.”

There is a second group owed the same thanks: our partners and community supporters. They've helped financially, donated time and expertise, improved our facilities, and provided services, equipment, technology, photography, food and training. When you see their signs, take a picture and remember their names — and when your family needs what they provide, give them the chance to earn your business. That is what a strong community does. We support the people who support us.

Four
Leadership

We need to be leaders in this community — not talking about gratitude, but demonstrating it. That is what Forge Gives Back is for. Every year the club will get behind a not-for-profit like ourselves, and it will never be about writing a check. It's about showing up.

“Leadership means inspiring others through your actions and example. Forge Gives Back is our action.”

This year: the Walk to End Alzheimer's

Every Forge game, practice and activity is canceled that day, because the club is treating the walk as our game day. In Mike's words: “Register, put it in your calendar, show up early, and be there for your team and your community.”

Registering and taking part costs nothing. Mike's ask is that each athlete raises $100 for the cause — every team that meets the goal gets a pizza party, and the top boys and girls fundraising teams earn special-edition Forge gear for the whole squad.

Register, donate and see the standings →

Five
Indomitable Spirit

By Mike's own admission a hundred-dollar word, and not an easy one to explain to a seven-year-old. Simply: unbreakable determination. One hundred percent effort, all the time.

It's what the club asks of its leadership, of its coaches and of its athletes — and what the organization commits to giving families in return. Work hard. Compete. Make mistakes. Learn. Get better. Together.

“We've written a story about how it started. But the Forge story is about what all of us choose to build next.”

Hello Forge families, Mike Weyant here. Today is a very exciting day. Today, we begin the inaugural season as Forge. There's tremendous energy and excitement as we've witnessed the preseason camp and more recently the Forge Fest. I want to take a quick minute just to bring everything home. Forge is so much more than a soccer club. We're trying to build an environment for our community's families to develop the character traits that will make these kids not only successful on the field, but in life. Let's talk about a few of them as it relates to the club and its on-goings. These aren't just words on paper. This is the beginning of the story about how we exemplify these characteristics that we want to instill in our children.

Let's talk about perseverance. Look at how far we've come. What we start today didn't exist a year ago. We had two clubs, two histories, two cultures, two sets of staff, and two sets of facilities, and a ton of people who weren't entirely sure this could be done. Frankly, there were a lot of moments in the process I wasn't sure this could be done. It was mission impossible. Many people told me not to attempt this. Together, we said challenge accepted. Perseverance means that you don't quit something because it's difficult. You keep going because it's worth doing. Today, we're one valley, one club, we're Forge and it's just the start.

Humility. Are we the best versions of ourselves right now at this moment? Yes, we could argue that. But can we do better? Absolutely. Did we get everything right? Absolutely not. We have made mistakes and we're going to make more. But humility means being willing to learn, willing to listen, willing to admit when you can do better. If we are humble and willing to learn and make mistakes, we can have the courage to do big things. We've added a ton of new staff. We're adding futsal programming. We've added 6v6 leagues. We're adding strength, agility and quickness training. We're adding nutritional programming for kids and adults. We're adding adult fitness. We're adding tutoring and homework help for supporting the academics. And we have the Forge Classic, among many other things. The organization is working really hard to create more opportunities for our athletes and families. The question quickly becomes, what will this club become if we are willing to build it?

Third is a big one and my personal favorite, gratitude. Let's talk about the power of gratitude. We would all be happier if we had a few minutes each day to be thankful and grateful. Teach your kids this. Somebody has made sacrifices they don't understand. I want every parent, grandparent or person who is supporting their kids athletes participation at Forge to know that we are extremely grateful at Forge. The kids owe you a huge hug. Go ask for it. Seriously. Take some time. Tell them they owe you a hug. Somebody is driving them. Someone bought the cleats. Someone got the 7am game assignment. Someone arranged for the team party. So anyone listening to this, thank you for trusting us to build something special for your families. None of this exists without you.

On the topic of gratitude, there's another group that deserves some recognition. To our partners and community supporters. You've helped us financially. You've donated your time, your expertise. You're helping us improve our facilities. You're providing services, equipment, technology, photography, food, training and so on. Families, when you see their signs, take a picture, remember their names. When your family needs something that they provide, give them an opportunity to earn your business. That's what a strong community does. We support the people who support us.

Let's talk about leadership. We need to be leaders in this community. We don't just talk about gratitude. We are a community demonstrating it. Forge gives back. This is launched this year. Every year we're going to give back to a not-for-profit organization like ourselves. This isn't about writing a check. It's about showing up.

This year we're supporting the Alzheimer's Association and the Walk to End Alzheimer's. We have canceled all Forge games, practices and activities that day because we're going to treat this walk as our game day. Register, put it in your calendar, show up early and be there for your team and your community. It does not cost anything to register and participate. We ask that each athlete raises $100 for the cause. For every team that meets that goal, the team will receive a pizza party. The top boys and girls fundraising teams are going to earn special edition Forge gear for the entire team. Leadership means inspiring others through your actions and example. Forge gives back is our action.

Number five, indomitable spirit. That's a $100 word for sure and this one won't be easy to explain to your seven-year-old. But let me make it simple. It just means unbreakable determination. It means 100% effort all the time.

That's what we're asking of the Forge leadership. That's what we're asking of our coaches. That's what we're asking of our athletes. And that's what this organization is going to give to you. We're going to work hard. We're going to compete. We're going to make mistakes. We're going to learn. We're going to get better, but we're going to do it together. We've written a story about how it started, but the Forge story is about what all of us choose to build next. Thank you for the few minutes and let's get a great 2026 and 27 season off to a start.

Fox Valley Forge SC
